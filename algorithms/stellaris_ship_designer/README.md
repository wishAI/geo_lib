# Stellaris · EQN-GO Mod Maker

This local-only sandbox previews editable browser conversions of assets from the user's installed copy of Stellaris on TK2 and the user's custom PonyLumen hero. It does not contain generated stand-in ship geometry.

## Stellaris Heart

The default design is the user's mint-and-lavender alicorn with fully opaque, non-crystal materials, restored custom cutie marks, metallic yellow details, hidden unused teeth/tongue geometry, and a head-following horn muzzle. This is the known-safe fallback after a whole-body translucent pass made the overlapping rounded torso appear hollow or absent in-game. The Materials pane independently configures gold, wings, eyes, cyan body/horn, and purple mane/tail: opacity, metalness, roughness, clearcoat, transmission, optional crystal shell, and faceted normals. Faceting changes normals without changing the silhouette. Optional shells follow the vanilla space-creature pattern: a stable base silhouette plus a separate `PdxMeshTerraAlphaBlend` edge shell. The WebGUI approximates the native shell with transmission, clearcoat, and sheen. Body length is 85% of the installed Mammalian S3 corvette's mesh-space body length (11.1299 versus 13.0940 units); wings, horn, hair, collision geometry, and turrets are excluded from this comparison.

The EQN-GO gameplay compiler uses the editable Heart JSON as its source of truth: 64 combat horn weapons (4 T, 12 X, 16 L, 12 M, 8 S, 8 P, 4 G), one fixed W planet-killer horn, 24 large utilities and 12 auxiliaries. Ten combat technologies and two incarnation technologies form the progression. Three guarded body identities can each exist as a space hero or a transportable assault army. Awakening stays at the capital; form switching uses a planetary decision on any owned colony in the body's system. The ground form can then embark and invade a hostile planet through the native army order. Ordinary shipyard construction is disabled to prevent the ground form from bypassing the cap. The W weapon uses a dedicated downward 45-degree idle-flight pose. Its charge is five times faster than the vanilla World Cracker, while the full firing sequence remains visible and explicit weapon-level timing prevents an instant order.

The Species tab contains exactly two empire-creation choices: Robot Eevee (8 portrait variants) and Krys Pony (78 file entries plus bonus Cadance). Each is a native portrait group whose members vary across leaders, rulers and populations; individual images are not separate species choices. The GUI selects the species preview image. Existing leaf portrait IDs remain hidden compatibility entries for previously created empires. There are 85 unique images overall. Sources came from the newer Mac `Documents/Paradox Interactive/Stellaris/mod/roboteevee` folder; the sibling ZIP was older. The NAS archive at `/home/wishAI/Backup/Games/stellaris custom mod/roboteevee.zip` is byte-identical to that older ZIP (24,323,878 bytes, SHA-256 `c99f6683b7e4d1888b4f9e512be4363873d25c532d7e28be6a86b2888ce12c5b`) and contributes no additional unique portraits. The broken legacy Krys 30 reference is repaired, and vanilla ROBOT/PLANT overrides are replaced with namespaced classes, modern portrait sets and AI/pre-FTL exclusions. The GUI supports portrait import, enabling/disabling images, species fields, origin, technologies, ground stats, commander traits, native animation bindings, build and deployment. Optional narrative events remain editable proposals and are never emitted.

`python3 algorithms/stellaris_ship_designer/mod_builder.py` writes the mod folder, portable ZIP and static-validation report under `outputs/`. The GUI Build / Deploy tab saves and validates its JSON before running the compiler. TK2 deployment uses the isolated code workspace and installs the generated local mod with a previous-release backup; it does not modify the active playset. Load EQN-GO after ACOT, Secrets Beyond the Gates and Override; disable legacy Roboteevee. Standalone mode omits ACOT dependencies and gates. The installed SBTG descriptor targets 4.1 while TK2 runs 4.4.6, so runtime evidence is reported separately from export checks.

Princess traits affect the fleet or army she actually commands. The origin always makes Stellaris Heart the country ruler, while each physical body has a separate commander representation of the same royal mind. The ruler receives the primary immortal Heart trait; commander-only progression remains on the physical-body leaders so Stellaris does not reject an extra ordinary ruler trait after its normal selections are full. Ground technology bonuses use one triggered commander army modifier scaled by a native script value for the highest researched stage. This follows the installed game’s shroud-trait pattern; direct army add_modifier effects are invalid in this build. The final technology is not gated behind the boss's Omega reward, and the boss's four scripted lives remain intact. Nominal damage/HP estimates are not proof of a solo win.

Awakening or rebuilding a missing body costs 10,000 alloys. The origin's initial body is free. Both transformation directions detach the commander before removing the old body, preserving identity, level and experience. Each body is counted across ships and armies, so transformations cannot create a fourth incarnation.

`build_heart.py` reconstructs the creator's original independent part rigs and attachment roots from the public `.bm` inputs. It preserves original inverse binds, rebases reflected source bone bases onto Blender's actual rest bases, restores original skin weights, and reproduces the omitted custom cutie marks from the saved projection controls. Do not merge same-named roots from separate part rigs or use the SMD Euler values as authoritative bind matrices.

Idle is the creator's original Fly, frames 25–48 at 24 FPS. The previous moving clip is a faster adaptation; dash is an authored forward-leaning variant. `planet_killer` reuses the verified idle flight while pitching the whole rig downward by the editable `animation.planetKillerPitch` value (−45 degrees by default). The native entity binds both `working` states to it and fires the W-slot beam from the same horn locator. Three selectable community candidates are decoded from user-supplied binary-5/model-18 DMX files: **Fast flight A · native VA body + wings**, **Fast flight B · Genmaxx**, and **Fast flight C · Argodaemon**. Fast flight C is the default editor preview and the native entity’s `moving` and `combat_moving` animation; idle remains the creator’s Fly. `inputs/stellaris_heart.json` records these bindings in `animation.gameStateMap`. Every clip has its own provenance label. Runtime hair jiggle physics, independent feather controls, and facial morph animation are not reproduced. The fast-flight clips use the existing three skinned tail joints for authored backward flow and follow-through; no added joints or weight changes were needed. Heart's verified left-ear rest transform replaces the malformed community snapshot channel in A, B, and C, while the correct right-ear motion remains untouched. The original idle, previous moving and dash clips remain unchanged. Accessories follow the retargeted attachment bones. The exported body preserves the selected zero facial morphs.

`dmx_flight.py` uses the external MIT Blender Source Tools decoder in `helper_repos/blender_source_tools/datamodel.py`. Animation root channels are already Y-up/+Z-forward, while the saved skeleton snapshot is Z-up/+X-forward and is not a neutral bind. Shared body bones use a local -90° Z basis conversion with Heart’s joint offsets preserved; source scene placement is removed so every community clip remains an in-place ship loop. A maps its native seven-beat Wing1 → Wing2 → Wing3 chain to Heart’s three large-wing bones; B and C use their native WingOpen → WingOpen0 → WingOpen02 chains. Source wing rotations are transferred as bone-local deltas on top of the PonyLumen large-wing rest transform rather than replacing it. The Motion pane exposes live body duration/speed, wing cycle/speed/phase, tail cycle/phase, Reset, and seamless-loop locking. Those values persist in `inputs/stellaris_heart.json`, immediately rebuild the WebGUI action, and drive the native Blender rebuild. The source body/wing samples receive endpoint correction when seamless locking is enabled. Exact bone pairs, source hashes, timing, loop closure and matrix checks are recorded in `outputs/heart/conversion.json`.

Rebuild and validate with the local Blender and existing `helper_repos/io_pdx_mesh`:

```sh
blender --background --factory-startup --python algorithms/stellaris_ship_designer/build_heart.py -- --source algorithms/stellaris_ship_designer/inputs/heart_source --output algorithms/stellaris_ship_designer/outputs/heart
blender --background --factory-startup --python algorithms/stellaris_ship_designer/export_heart.py -- --blend algorithms/stellaris_ship_designer/outputs/heart/heart.blend --output algorithms/stellaris_ship_designer/outputs/heart/native
blender --background --factory-startup --python algorithms/stellaris_ship_designer/validate_heart.py -- --blend algorithms/stellaris_ship_designer/outputs/heart/heart.blend --native algorithms/stellaris_ship_designer/outputs/heart/native
```

## Included source examples

- **Mammalian Battleship** — the original XL1 bow, L3 core, L1 stern, five-bone frame, 39 locators, and the shipped `idle`, `death`, `death2`, and `death3` actions.
- **BioGenesis Mauler, growth stage 1** — the original skinned mesh, 16-bone hierarchy, 10 embedded locators, one source-defined `root` slot-origin marker, and the shipped `idle`, `combat_moving`, and `attack` action files. The installed `biogenesis_01_ships.gfx` binds idle and combat movement. Its attack binding is commented out, so the designer labels that clip **shipped but disabled**.

The Mauler mesh contains `weapon_01` and `weapon_02`, but the installed `MAULER_STAGE_1_swarm` section binds both weapon components to `locatorname = "root"` through invisible turret templates. The designer therefore shows `root` as the official slot origin and labels the two mesh weapon locators as embedded but unbound. Locator XYZ lines are local transform axes, not projectile paths; the turret and projectile definitions determine firing direction.

The optional **Laser test** is a sandbox visualization, not an extracted game action. Battleship muzzle markers animate a cyan shot along their pose-derived local `+Z` rest direction. The Mauler stage-1 `root` binding has no fixed direction in the section source, so its test displays an expanding origin pulse until a firing axis is explicitly chosen. Distant target, engine, exhaust, and XL wind-up markers remain preserved but are hidden by default; selecting any locator temporarily reveals its exact source pose.

The BioGenesis size definition identifies the Mauler stage 1 as a `bio_ship`, gives it `fleet_slot_size = 1`, and upgrades it to stage 2. The swarm section template has torpedo and small-gun component slots, two small utility slots, and one auxiliary slot.

## Provenance and storage

The PDX `.mesh` and `.anim` files were decoded with `io_pdx_mesh` commit `170a4cba0c272c3825e79bbba244796fdc746e92`, then exported as GLB with their original skinning, hierarchy, locator nodes, texture maps, and actions intact. Collision-only geometry was removed for the browser preview. Battleship section locators were parented to their corresponding original frame bones so they follow the shipped death actions.

The vanilla and custom Heart GLBs are intentionally excluded from Git. `large_files.json` declares their exact byte sizes and SHA-256 hashes under the project Nextcloud asset store, while the repository input paths are stable symlinks created by `./geo storage hydrate`.

Only use the extracted assets with a legitimately installed copy of Stellaris and subject to the game's applicable terms.
